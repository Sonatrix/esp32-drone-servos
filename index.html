    How does a servo know where to point?

    The ESP32 sends a tiny pulse 50 times every second. The length of each pulse is the message: short = turn one way, long = turn the other way.

    🎯 Try this: drag the slider and watch only the width of the high pulse change, while the 20 ms period stays the same. Then switch servo type: the same angle needs a different pulse!

    What is inside a servo?

    A servo is a motor + gears + a position sensor + a tiny brain. The brain keeps comparing where the horn is to where you asked it to be, and pushes the motor until the error is zero.

    🎯 Try this: grab the white horn and drag it away, then let go: it fights back! Next, crank Kp up to 12 and watch it overshoot and wobble. Turn Kp down and it gets lazy. Real servo makers tune this balance.

    A servo that keeps the camera level

    When a drone tilts, a gimbal servo turns the opposite way so the camera keeps looking at the horizon. The ESP32 reads the tilt (IMU) and sets the servo angle.

    📌 ESP32 pin notes

    Good servo signal pins
    GPIO 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 23, 25, 26, 27, 32, 33 (any output-capable pin works with LEDC PWM).
    Avoid for servos
    GPIO 0, 2, 12, 15 (boot/strapping pins can glitch at start-up), GPIO 6–11 (flash), GPIO 34–39 (input only).
    3.3 V signal is fine
    The ESP32 outputs 3.3 V logic. Most servos accept it on the signal wire even though they run on 5 V. If one twitches, add a level shifter.
    Timers & channels
    ESP32Servo uses the LEDC PWM hardware: up to 16 channels, 4 timers. Call ESP32PWM::allocateTimer() first.
    MPU6050 IMU
    VCC→3V3, GND→GND, SDA→GPIO21, SCL→GPIO22 (I²C). Keep it away from motors and vibration-isolate it.
    Power
    5 V BEC for servos. Power the ESP32 from the same battery via its own regulator or the BEC's VIN, never from a servo wire.

    🎯 Try this: compare all four options. Only one is right for a real drone: a separate 5 V supply for the servos, with grounds joined so the signal has a reference.

    Quadcopters: where are the servos?

    A typical quadcopter has no servos. It steers by spinning its four motors at different speeds. Each motor is driven by an ESC (electronic speed controller) that reads the same kind of 50 Hz pulse a servo does, but 1000 µs = stop and 2000 µs = full power.

    ServoESC + motor
    Pulse500–2500 µs1000–2000 µs
    Rate50 Hz50 Hz (or faster)
    Outputan anglea speed (3-phase power)
    Powerup to ~2 A10–40 A per motor

    🎯 Try this: raise the throttle before arming: nothing happens, ESCs ignore you until armed. Arm at zero throttle, then add throttle. Now push Roll right: the two right motors slow down and the left ones speed up. No servos needed!
